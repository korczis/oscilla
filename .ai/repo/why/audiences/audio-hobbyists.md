# People measuring their own speakers and rooms

## Who this is

Somebody who wants to know how their laptop speaker and microphone shape sound
(`measure-a-playback-capture-chain-response`), whether a foam pad under a desk speaker changes
the top end (`record-a-finding-linked-to-evidence`), or what the background noise in their room
looks like band by band (`run-a-third-octave-rta`). Some own a measurement microphone with a
correction file and a 94 dB SPL calibrator (`calibrate-a-microphone-frequency-response`).

## How they work

At home, one setup at a time, changing one part of the chain and measuring again. They compare
curves by eye and draw conclusions from the difference.

## What goes wrong

A room curve read as the speaker's response. A clipped or noisy sweep that draws a curve as
smooth as a good one. A level read as dB SPL without a calibration. A comparison of two curves
whose settings differed in a way nobody recorded.
