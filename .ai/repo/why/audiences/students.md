# Students learning about sound and hearing

## Who this is

Somebody learning acoustics, signals or hearing who wants to try the idea rather than read
about it: what a low-pass filter does to a tone a decade above its cutoff
(`low-pass-a-tone`), how a square wave is built from odd harmonics
(`build-a-tone-from-harmonics`), whether a dog or a bat could hear a tone
(`compare-a-frequency-with-animal-hearing`).

## How they work

On whatever machine and speakers are at hand, often a laptop, sometimes from a downloaded file.
They take the numbers on the screen at face value, because they do not yet know which ones a
browser can and cannot know.

## What goes wrong

A level that looks like a sound pressure and is not. A statement about what a frequency does to
a person or an animal that no source supports. A result that cannot be explained afterwards
because nothing recorded how it was made.
